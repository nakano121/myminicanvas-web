## Why the chunky style matters

Not all colouring pages are built for little hands. The delicate, intricate ones — mandalas, fine florals — are lovely for older children, but they set a toddler up to "fail": lines too thin to stay inside, spaces too small for a crayon tip.

These are the opposite on purpose:

- **Thick, bold outlines** that read clearly and hide the crayon that strays.
- **Big open shapes** — a round belly, a fluffy mane — that fill up fast, so a two-year-old feels finished before they lose interest.
- **One friendly character per page**, centered, with white space around it. Calm, not cluttered.

Colouring is quietly doing real work while it looks like play: it builds the pencil grip and hand strength that later become handwriting, and it's one of the few screen-free activities that reliably settles a wound-up child.
